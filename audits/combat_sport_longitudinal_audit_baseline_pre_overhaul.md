# Non-MMA longitudinal development audit

## Method

- Four fresh-world seeds: `101`, `202`, `303`, and `404`.
- Fifteen simulated years per seed, with snapshots at years 3, 5, 10, and 15.
- Five production circuits: Boxing, Kickboxing, Muay Thai (including Lethwei athletes), Wrestling, and Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu.
- Each simulated month calls the production `process_combat_sport_worlds` path. Each year boundary calls production `age_world_one_year` in the same order used by the game calendar.
- The audit tracks every original athlete and generated prospect through rating changes, bouts, peak rating/age, retirement, crossover exit, and circuit replenishment.
- No save was loaded or written. Core game files were not edited.

The machine-readable source is `audits/combat_sport_longitudinal_audit_latest.json`; the reusable runner is `tools/combat_sport_longitudinal_audit.py`.

Seed-to-seed variation was small. The standard deviation of the four seed-level year-15 original-cohort rating changes was about 0.07-0.11 points in every sport, so the direction and scale are structural rather than a lucky seed.

The fall in activity by year 15 (2.31-2.87 bouts per original athlete-year) is caused by original athletes retiring, not by inactive active fighters. Rotation coverage was complete in the first ten years: no original athlete had zero new bouts.

Headcount replenishment is stable: year-15 active rosters remain at 89.5%-90.5% of their starting sizes. The system reacts after retirements begin, adding up to three prospects per month until the 88% threshold is cleared. It does not create an advance development pipeline during the first five years.

Quality is not preserved. Generated prospects enter 40.8%-44.9% below the original mean rating, depending on sport. With almost the entire original cohort retired by year 15, the active circuit rating falls 39.3%-43.4% from its starting level.

Generated prospects gained only 1.45-1.72 rating points to their observed peak during this run. Their observed mean peak age of 23.2-23.6 is right-censored: many were generated late in the 15-year horizon and had not completed a career. It should not be interpreted as a true career peak.

Crossovers did not materially explain roster loss: four seeds produced zero Boxing, Wrestling, and BJJ crossovers; 0.25 Kickboxing and 0.75 Muay Thai crossovers per seed by year 15.

## Anomalies

### Critical: Boxing suffers a one-time broad-skill normalization loss

Boxing alone falls roughly 13 rating points by year 3 and never recovers, while every other sport gains. A focused first-month probe showed individual losses of 13-15 points as authored broad `striking` ratings around 90 were reset to detailed-skill standing averages around 78-83.

The trigger is camp/detail development: `apply_gym_camp_micro_improvement` changes one detailed skill and then calls `sync_broad_skills_from_details`, which recomputes every broad skill. The real Boxing profiles' authored broad ratings and detailed-skill averages are not aligned. Examples in seed 101 included Juan Francisco Estrada dropping from 213.69 to 198.69 and Kazuto Ioka from 210.10 to 195.10 after their first resync.

### Critical: roster quantity is preserved but competitive quality collapses

The replacement generation range is far below the real starting rosters. By year 15, active ratings are 78-100 points lower than at game start despite healthy headcounts. This makes every circuit progressively become a low-tier generated league rather than sustaining a believable world standard.

### High: synchronized age and retirement cliff

Starting age means are 28.64-29.20 with P10-P90 almost universally 27-31. Retirement reviews begin at 38; the resulting retirement median is 39 in every sport. Between years 10 and 15, nearly every real-name athlete disappears at once. A broader initial age pyramid and/or a wider retirement-age curve is needed for organic turnover.

### Medium: development is shallow

Outside the Boxing resync, five-year original-cohort gains range from only +0.81 to +3.71 sport-rating points. The results are stable across seeds and peak gains remain +1.72 to +4.65. The system produces small movement, but not much separation between high- and low-development careers.

### Medium: activity is uniform and high for some sports

The rotation logic succeeds at avoiding forgotten athletes, but Boxing, Kickboxing, Wrestling, and BJJ original athletes average about four bouts per year. That is aggressive for modern elite boxing and unusually uniform across sports. Muay Thai's lower figure of about three is primarily explained by its 70-person roster sharing the same monthly card cap, rather than a sport-specific activity model.

### Informational: sport rating scales are not normalized

Starting means range from 198.01 in Kickboxing to 236.49 in BJJ. This is expected from the distinct weight formulas, but any UI or cross-sport comparison should avoid presenting them as a shared universal rating scale.

## Positive findings

- Monthly card rotation reached every original athlete; zero-fight incidence was 0% through year 10.
- Replenishment reliably held active roster quantity near its designed threshold across every seed and sport.
- Retirement-pending limbo was negligible at checkpoints (0-0.5 athlete per circuit at year 10 and zero at year 15).
- Multi-seed variance was low, making the main findings reproducible.
