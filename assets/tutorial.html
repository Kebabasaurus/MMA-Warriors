Getting Started

You run an MMA promotion in a persistent, simulated combat-sports world alongside a full roster of AI-controlled companies, a regional development pipeline that feeds new talent into every major promotion, and a free-agent market that never stops moving — whether you're watching or not.

The left-hand navigation

Screens are grouped into four sections you'll use constantly:

  • Today — what needs your attention this week.
  • Promotion — everything about running your own company.
  • World — the wider industry: rivals, rankings, and the talent pipeline.
  • Tools — saves, rules, and testing utilities.
Advance Week (top-right of every screen) moves the game forward one week. This is the core loop: check your Assistant and Inbox, book or adjust your card, then advance. Monthly business (development, retirements, market churn, regional graduations) runs automatically as weeks roll over into a new month.

Assistant

Weekly Command Centre — the next decisions, show readiness, runway, division health, and attributed changes. This is the first screen to check every week: it tells you what actually needs a decision instead of making you hunt across every tab.

Inbox

Mail / Decisions — owner goals, decisions, contract alerts, suspensions, and business mail. Anything requiring a yes/no from you (title-shot clauses, retirement reviews, contract expirations) surfaces here.

Media Desk

Website — build the company brand, promote events, manage rights, and follow the fight world. Popularity and stability both respond to how well you're promoting your shows here, not just the fights themselves.

Fight Night

Live Fight Night — the blow-by-blow report and office news for whatever card just ran, yours or the wider world's.

Roster

Company Roster — sort fighters, check status, and build your divisions. Every signed fighter's contract, form, and readiness lives here.

A fighter stranded in a division you've closed can be released from their profile via Cut From Roster — severance is paid automatically based on their purse and any remaining guaranteed fights.

Matchmaking

Add Show / Matchmaking — build the card from opener to main event. The available-fighters table is colour-coded so you can spot a viable card at a glance:

ColourMeaning
GreenWinning record
RedLosing record
GreyUnavailable on this date (injured, fatigued, or already booked)

Hover over any column header for a tooltip explaining what it measures — Title Path and Form columns mirror what you'd see on the Rankings screen, so you can judge a matchup's stakes without leaving this tab.

Contracts

Contracts — expiring deals, renewal talks, and roster wage pressure. Negotiate early: a fighter who runs out their deal becomes a free agent the wider market can bid on too.

Free Agents

Free Agents — scout talent and negotiate new contracts. This pool includes regional graduates, released fighters, and long-time journeymen alike — check age, recent form, and potential before committing to a deal, not just the headline overall rating.

Scouting

Scouting — evaluate fighters, observe upcoming bouts, search regions, and turn uncertain reports into recruitment decisions. Recommendations here are advisory: they combine projected ability, potential, market pull, your divisional depth, asking price, and available cash, but the final call is always yours.

Fight Academy

Build an in-house development academy (an initial investment, roughly $180,000) to grow your own prospects from scratch rather than relying purely on the free-agent market. Paid facility upgrades speed up graduate development further once it's running.

Staff

Staff / Scouting / Drug Testing — hire staff, assign scouting, manage testing, and set post-show bonuses.

Finance

Finance — ticketing, broadcast income, sponsorship, payroll, production, medical, tax, and the full ledger. Check this before committing to big signings or venue upgrades.

World Hub

World — promotions, news, market churn, and the wider MMA economy. This is the pulse of everything happening outside your own company.

Regional Prospects

Every region runs its own development circuit — fifteen feeder promotions worldwide, each running real developmental cards every month. Fighters build genuine in-ring form there before they're ever eligible to enter the wider free-agent market: eligibility is earned through actual bouts fought, wins, momentum, and age, not just a fabricated backstory record. Browse this tab to see who's Eligible Now, Nearly Eligible, or still Developing — and to understand exactly what a prospect still needs before they're ready.

Fighter Search

Fighter Search — search the full combat-sports world and inspect recent form for anyone, signed or unsigned.

Companies

Industry Standings — every promotion and combat-sport circuit, ranked by power. Filter, sort, and open a profile to see exactly why a rival is rated where it is.

Rankings

Rankings — division rankings and pound-for-pound rankings across the whole sport.

Results

Results Database — event recaps, critical ratings, commercial ratings, gates, methods, and full show histories.

Regions

Game World — regions, states, economies, legal status, drug testing rules, teams, and local show history.

Game & Saves

Save, load, start a new game, and export or load a fighter database. Quick Save and Quick Load are always available at the bottom of the left-hand navigation too.

Company Rules

Company Editor — belts, rules, broadcasters, and weight classes for your promotion.

World Editor

Edit the current career directly, or maintain the reusable starting universe used for future new games.

Sim Lab

Simulation Lab — division-aware fight testing, scouting cards, bracket simulations, and engine audits. Useful for testing hypothetical matchups without touching your real save.

Small Promotion Survival

Popularity and stability grow from more than just winning. Hype, excitement, attendance relative to your venue's capacity, and turning a profit all feed into it — a small promotion below 40 popularity even gets a helping hand to keep growth from stalling out entirely. Don't judge your trajectory off one bad night.
Watch fighter fit, not just rating. A new signing takes time to settle into a division — the fit penalty fades as they get more bouts under their belt with you.
Don't sleep on Regional Prospects. A steady trickle of genuinely proven, affordable fighters comes out of the regional circuits every month — it's usually a better value signing than chasing an established free agent your budget can't really support yet.
Check Contracts before it's urgent. A lapsed deal doesn't just cost you the fighter — it hands them straight to the open market where any rival can make an offer.
