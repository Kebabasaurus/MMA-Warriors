Getting Started

You run an MMA promotion in a persistent, simulated combat-sports world alongside a full roster of AI-controlled companies, a regional development pipeline that feeds new talent into every major promotion, and a free-agent market that never stops moving — whether you're watching or not.

The left-hand navigation

Screens are grouped into four sections you'll use constantly:

  • Today — what needs your attention this week.
  • Promotion — everything about running your own company.
  • World — the wider industry: rivals, rankings, and the talent pipeline.
  • Tools — saves, rules, and testing utilities.
Advance Week (top-right of every screen) moves the game forward one week. This is the core loop: check your Assistant and Inbox, book or adjust your card, then advance. Monthly business (development, retirements, market churn, regional graduations) runs automatically as weeks roll over into a new month.

Assistant

Weekly Command Centre — the next decisions, show readiness, runway, division health, and attributed changes. This is the first screen to check every week: it tells you what actually needs a decision instead of making you hunt across every tab.

Inbox

Mail / Decisions — owner goals, decisions, contract alerts, suspensions, and business mail. Anything requiring a yes/no from you (title-shot clauses, retirement reviews, contract expirations) surfaces here.

Media Desk

Website — build the company brand, promote events, manage rights, and follow the fight world. Popularity and stability both respond to how well you're promoting your shows here, not just the fights themselves.

Fight Night

Live Fight Night — the blow-by-blow report and office news for whatever card just ran, yours or the wider world's.

Roster

Company Roster — sort fighters, check status, and build your divisions. Every signed fighter's contract, form, and readiness lives here.

A fighter stranded in a division you've closed can be released from their profile via Cut From Roster — severance is paid automatically based on their purse and any remaining guaranteed fights.

Matchmaking

Add Show / Matchmaking — build the card from opener to main event. The available-fighters table is colour-coded so you can spot a viable card at a glance:

ColourMeaning
GreenWinning record
RedLosing record
GreyUnavailable on this date (injured, fatigued, or already booked)

Hover over any column header for a tooltip explaining what it measures — Title Path and Form columns mirror what you'd see on the Rankings screen, so you can judge a matchup's stakes without leaving this tab.

Crossover Superfight Night — a special, player-only event type: pay an extra sanctioning fee to book a champion from your promotion against a champion from a rival promotion. Non-title, so nobody's belt changes hands, and only close-enough weight classes can be matched — but it can headline a whole card of its own, with regular prelims underneath.

Contracts

Contracts — expiring deals, renewal talks, and roster wage pressure. Negotiate early: a fighter who runs out their deal becomes a free agent the wider market can bid on too.

Two negotiable clauses matter beyond pay: a title-shot clause guarantees a fighter a crack at the belt within their deal, and guaranteed comeback fights keep a fighter locked to fulfilling those bouts even through a retirement review — they get their remaining scheduled fights before any retirement takes effect.

Free Agents

Free Agents — scout talent and negotiate new contracts. This pool includes regional graduates, released fighters, and long-time journeymen alike — check age, recent form, and potential before committing to a deal, not just the headline overall rating.

Scouting

Scouting — evaluate fighters, observe upcoming bouts, search regions, and turn uncertain reports into recruitment decisions. Recommendations here are advisory: they combine projected ability, potential, market pull, your divisional depth, asking price, and available cash, but the final call is always yours.

Fight Academy

Build an in-house development academy (an initial investment, roughly $180,000) to grow your own prospects from scratch rather than relying purely on the free-agent market. Amateurs train, fight amateur showcases, and eventually graduate into a real professional contract once they're ready — the academy workspace tracks each prospect's readiness so you know who's close. Paid facility upgrades speed up development further once it's running, letting a serious talent pipeline pay for itself over time instead of relying purely on what the free-agent market happens to offer.

Staff

Staff / Scouting / Drug Testing — hire staff, assign scouting, manage testing, and set post-show bonuses.

Finance

Finance — ticketing, broadcast income, sponsorship, payroll, production, medical, tax, and the full ledger. Check this before committing to big signings or venue upgrades.

World Hub

World — promotions, news, market churn, and the wider MMA economy. This is the pulse of everything happening outside your own company.

Regional Prospects

Every region runs its own development circuit — fifteen feeder promotions worldwide, each running real developmental cards every month. Fighters build genuine in-ring form there before they're ever eligible to enter the wider free-agent market: eligibility is earned through actual bouts fought, wins, momentum, and age, not just a fabricated backstory record. Browse this tab to see who's Eligible Now, Nearly Eligible, or still Developing — and to understand exactly what a prospect still needs before they're ready.

Fighter Search

Fighter Search — search the full combat-sports world and inspect recent form for anyone, signed or unsigned.

Combat Sports

Combat Sports Universe — MMA isn't the only sport running: boxing, kickboxing, BJJ, and more each have their own AI flagship promotion. Open your own child promotion in any of them (for example a BJJ arm under your main brand), sign that sport's athletes, and book and run its cards separately from your MMA business. Academy prospects who don't fit your MMA roster can graduate straight into an open child promotion instead.

Companies

Industry Standings — every promotion and combat-sport circuit, ranked by power. Filter, sort, and open a profile to see exactly why a rival is rated where it is.

Rankings

Rankings — division rankings and pound-for-pound rankings across the whole sport.

Results

Results Database — event recaps, critical ratings, commercial ratings, gates, methods, and full show histories.

Regions

Game World — regions, states, economies, legal status, drug testing rules, teams, and local show history.

Game & Saves

Save, load, start a new game, and export or load a fighter database. Quick Save and Quick Load are always available at the bottom of the left-hand navigation too.

Company Rules

Company Editor — belts, rules, broadcasters, and weight classes for your promotion.

World Editor

Edit the current career directly, or maintain the reusable starting universe used for future new games.

Sim Lab

Simulation Lab — division-aware fight testing, scouting cards, bracket simulations, and engine audits. Useful for testing hypothetical matchups without touching your real save.

Small Promotion Survival

Popularity and stability grow from more than just winning. Hype, excitement, attendance relative to your venue's capacity, and turning a profit all feed into it — a small promotion below 40 popularity even gets a helping hand to keep growth from stalling out entirely. Don't judge your trajectory off one bad night.
Watch fighter fit, not just rating. A new signing takes time to settle into a division — the fit penalty fades as they get more bouts under their belt with you.
Don't sleep on Regional Prospects. A steady trickle of genuinely proven, affordable fighters comes out of the regional circuits every month — it's usually a better value signing than chasing an established free agent your budget can't really support yet.
Check Contracts before it's urgent. A lapsed deal doesn't just cost you the fighter — it hands them straight to the open market where any rival can make an offer.

Spoilers — Stat Dynamics Explained

Simplified versions of the actual formulas running under the hood. Numbers can shift in future balance passes, but the shape of each mechanic won't.

⚠ Spoiler Warning

This reveals the actual formulas behind popularity, stability, regional graduation, show frequency, and free-agent retirement. Some players prefer to learn these by playing.

Are you sure?

Popularity & stability after a show

Both move by a small delta each time you run an event, rounded to the nearest whole point and clamped between 1 and 100:

  • Popularity delta ≈ (avg hype − 43) ⁄ 15 + (avg excitement − 44) ⁄ 16 + (regional pull − 1) × 1.6 + attendance ratio × 1.8 + (+0.4 if profitable, −0.7 if not)
  • Stability delta ≈ profit ⁄ 300,000 + (avg excitement − 40) ⁄ 24 + attendance ratio × 2.2 − 0.3 − mismatch penalty × 0.28

Attendance ratio is just attendance ÷ venue capacity — a sold-out small venue helps more than a half-empty big one. If your company popularity is below 40, popularity gets an extra boost that fades out as you approach 40, so a small promotion isn't stuck fighting a baseline built for a global juggernaut.

Regional eligibility — the seven pathways

A regional fighter needs real, in-engine bouts (never a fabricated backstory record) to satisfy any one of these before they count as Eligible Now:

PathwayRequirement
Early breakthroughAge 23+, 11+ bouts, potential 86+, and (7+ wins or momentum +4)
Proven regionalAge 25+, 12+ bouts, 7+ wins, 53%+ win rate
Established exitAge 27+, 15+ bouts, 51%+ win rate
Hot regional runStill with their original circuit, 7+ wins, momentum at the +5 cap, 26+ popularity
Circuit complete(Age 24+ and 22+ bouts) or 30+ bouts regardless of age
Veteran exitAge 28+, 15+ bouts
Aging outAge 29+, 7+ bouts, and results not already strong enough to justify staying

Younger than 18, injured, mid-retirement, or already retired always blocks eligibility outright, no matter which pathway is met.

How many actually graduate each month

Every feeder circuit runs a card every month — up to 15 bouts, spread fairly round-robin across every division with enough fighters, not dumped onto whichever division happens to go first. But graduating out of a circuit into free agency is separately budgeted, 0–6 slots per month across the whole world, driven by whichever pressure is highest:

  • Reserve pressure — thin free-agent market (under 170 usable free agents pushes this toward its max)
  • Demand pressure — how many jobs the major promotions have open
  • Backlog pressure — how large the Eligible Now pile already is, so a quiet market can't let readiness balloon forever unaddressed

That budget then rotates fairly across all fifteen circuits, so it isn't always the same one or two feeders supplying every graduate.

How often the AI runs shows

Each promotion rolls a monthly chance based on its "show personality" (Super Shows, Star Builder, Frequent Small Cards, and so on), adjusted by its financial mode, executive personality, board mandate, financial pressure, roster health, and roster depth — then that whole result is scaled down by 0.65 before being rolled against. A cash-rich, deep-rostered flagship promotion runs noticeably more often than a small one.

Emergency free-agent call-ups

If the active free-agent market ever crashes below 160, the game tries to refill it back toward 200 almost entirely from the regional circuits before ever inventing a brand-new fighter: it pulls evenly, round-robin, across all fifteen feeder circuits — no per-circuit cap — so a real crash is absorbed by genuine regional talent instead of diluting the world with fabricated fighters. Only whatever the regionals genuinely can't cover falls back to a freshly generated free agent.

Company power score & industry tier

Every promotion's overall standing is a single power score built from star power, champions held (capped), cash on hand, and roster depth. That score buckets into a tier: Global at 280+, National from 190–279, Regional from 100–189, and Local below 100. Climbing a tier is a real, visible milestone — not just a cosmetic label.

Settling into a new division

A fighter competing above their natural frame size starts with a real division-fit penalty. It eases every month they keep training and competing there — younger fighters adapt noticeably faster than veterans, and a bigger natural frame or better conditioning speeds it up further — until only a small residual penalty remains for a genuinely undersized fighter. This is why a new signing often looks better a year in than the day you signed them.

When a stagnant free agent retires

Twice a year, any unsigned free agent who is age 30+, rated under 55 overall, on a 3+ fight losing streak, with a win rate of 30% or less over their last 5 real fights, and not a genuine blue-chip prospect gets flagged for one final career-review fight before retiring. This exists purely to stop free agency filling up with fighters who are never going anywhere.

Matchmaking fit score

The 1–99 score shown in Matchmaking is a compressed, readable version of a much larger internal number that factors in hype, rivalry, and title stakes — the raw number is squeezed down and floored at 1 / capped at 99 so it stays comparable across every possible matchup, not a literal win probability.

Monthly growth & decline

Every signed and free-agent fighter gets a development roll each month. If it clears a random threshold and they're still under their potential (and within their prime growth window), they improve — bigger clearances produce bigger jumps, some months are a flat plateau, and young, low-rated prospects with real upside get an easier roll so genuine talent isn't stuck frozen for an entire career. Past their prime, a separate decline roll can chip skills away instead, and it hits harder the further past prime they are — durable archetypes resist it longest.
