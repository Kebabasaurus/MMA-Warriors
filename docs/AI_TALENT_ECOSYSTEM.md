# AI Talent Ecosystem Audit

## Regional Feeder Layer

Regional feeders are non-financial developmental circuits. They run low-cost development
cards and start fighters at age 16 or older. Their rosters favour ages 16-26, low current
popularity, modest current ability, and higher potential ceilings. A fighter graduates to
free agency when they build a meaningful record and show enough current ability or upside.
Fighters who do not immediately attract a major deal can be recruited back to a regional
circuit to stay active.

Current feeder promotions:

- Japan Fight Circuit
- UK Regional MMA
- North American Fighting League
- European Challenge MMA
- Asia Rising Championship

## Fighter Development Audit

All active promotion rosters now receive monthly development checks, even when their
promotion does not run a show. Development uses:

- Age relative to prime window.
- Potential headroom (`potential - overall`).
- Gym quality, facilities, specialties, capacity, and room crowding.
- Dedication, professionalism, trait, momentum, morale, fatigue, and injury.
- Yearly aging/decline and career record pressure.

Regional winners have an additional small breakthrough chance before graduation, while
high-potential fighters gain more value from gym and camp development than established stars.

## AI Signing Audit

AI promotions no longer choose a random free agent and immediately sign them. Each normal
promotion evaluates free agents using:

- Division depth need by gender and weight class.
- Current ability, potential, age, record momentum, morale, popularity, star quality, and media value.
- Regional fit.
- Contract cost versus promotion cash, operating reserve, reputation, and stability.

An eligible AI proposes a visible rival offer first. The offer records company, purse,
contract length, signing bonus, and a deadline in the following month. The player can open
the same fighter's market negotiation and beat the terms before resolution. On expiry, the
fighter accepts or rejects using the package, company reputation/stability, regional fit,
career upside, and variance. The signing then deducts the AI promotion's real cash.

This means a player can win a deal away from an AI, an AI can be rejected, and a promotion
that cannot afford the reserve plus commitment cannot make the offer.

## Player Academy and Multi-Sport Pathways

The player academy is a shared talent pipeline, rather than a separate academy for each
combat sport. A player can build it, hire a scout, and establish one regional youth network
at a time. Setup takes eight weeks. Once live, the network rolls for leads weekly until its
shortlist contains eight active prospects; unsigned leads expire after 2–3 weeks.

Prospects are generated in their network region at age 12–15, with 30–60 current rating.
Potential normally tops out at 98. A 99–100 potential prospect requires an elite scout,
academy reputation, and a deliberately low-probability generational roll. Scout quality
controls both prospect quality and the accuracy of the current/potential range shown before
signing.

Academy members train, compete on biweekly amateur cards, and can turn professional at 16.
Promotion at age 16–17 is explicitly confirmed as an early debut; 18 is the normal route.
At graduation the player chooses MMA or any opened child sport. The athlete's recommended
focus and preferred pathway guide the decision but do not override player control.

Child sports are branded player-owned promotions (for example `UFC BJJ`), not invisible
roster buckets. They begin empty, require deliberate signings, support player-booked
matchups and smart cards, and run their own titles, rankings, records, and finances. Their
investment, signing, and card profit/loss flow through the parent organisation's cash while
their own financial history remains visible.
