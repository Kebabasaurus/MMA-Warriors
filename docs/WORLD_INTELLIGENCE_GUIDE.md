# World Intelligence Guide

MMA Warriors uses persistent promotion identities so the wider world behaves like competing companies rather than a single generic card generator.

## Promotion Strategy

Every promotion has an identity, media voice, core priorities, risk tolerance, and a current strategy mode.

Core priorities are stable:

- `star_focus`: preference for recognisable draws and major-event value.
- `prospect_focus`: preference for younger upside and careful development.
- `merit_focus`: preference for rankings, form, and contender progression.

The current mode changes when the company’s situation changes:

- `Financial Recovery`: smaller, lower-risk cards when cash or stability is weak.
- `Prospect Rebuild`: development-oriented recruitment and protected matchmaking.
- `Contender Cycle`: rankings and divisional movement take priority.
- `Star Chasing`: the company leans into marketable fighters and major attractions.
- `Title Push`: champions and credible title challengers are emphasised.

AI free-agent valuations use the same priorities. A prospect builder values upside more than an established draw; a star-driven company makes the opposite calculation. Company profiles show the identity, current direction, and media voice.

## Staff Effects

Player staff have a specialty and reputation in addition to their role, skill, salary, and morale.

- Matchmakers improve the quality of matchmaking recommendations and the build score of booked fights.
- Marketing staff add modest hype to cards.
- Scouts affect the quality and upside of generated scouting discoveries.
- Talent Relations staff reduce negotiation friction.
- Doctors are represented in the staff profile and remain the next planned expansion point for recovery and medical decisions.

Effects are intentionally modest. Strong staff provide an edge, not deterministic outcomes.

## Negotiation Personas

Each fighter has an agent and a negotiation persona. The persona adds genuine priorities to negotiations:

- `Hard Bargainer`: money and clear terms.
- `Loyalist`: direction and respect on a renewal.
- `Star Chaser`: major compensation and revenue share.
- `Security First`: guaranteed fights and contract clarity.
- `Competitive`: a defined route to a title shot.
- `Professional`: a balanced package.

The negotiation screen explains the representative and personality, and unmet priorities feed its counter-offer feedback.

## Chronicle And Camps

The World Chronicle is a persistent, filterable historical record. It stores major strategy shifts, title changes, negotiated signings, regional breakthroughs, retirements, and completed player events. It is saved with the world, so a long-running universe accumulates a readable history instead of relying only on transient news.

Players can set a camp focus from the Roster screen before scheduling an event:

- `Striking`, `Wrestling`, `Grappling`, `Conditioning`, and `Game Plan` can produce a small focused detailed-skill improvement during a sufficiently long camp.
- `Weight Management` improves weigh-in preparation.
- Gym specialty fit improves a focus plan, but it never guarantees development.

A high-skill Doctor reduces the player roster’s recovery window after a fight. This is intentionally a small operational edge rather than a way to erase damage or overbook fighters.

## Media Desk And Promises

The Media Desk lets the player choose a roster spokesperson, issue a same-division callout, run an interview, or fund a press tour. These actions change media heat, popularity, rivalries, and therefore later fight-build value. Major media actions are also written into the World Chronicle.

Negotiations can now include main-event and top-opponent promises. They improve the offer for fighters who value opportunity, but must be fulfilled within six months. Completing the commitment builds trust and morale; missing it damages trust, morale, and future negotiation leverage.

## Camp Workload

Camp Plan includes a gym choice and workload:

- `Light` is lower-risk but offers less preparation.
- `Standard` is the neutral baseline.
- `Hard` can improve preparation, but carries a small camp-injury risk.
